How the letters are built

Each letter starts as a skeleton on a shared grid, and every part of that skeleton is then reshaped by the curve. Each switch under "What the shape changes" turns one of these steps on or off; Lock freezes a slider and keeps Randomize away from it.

  • Grid: baseline 0, cap-height and ascender 86, descender −28. With Proportions on, x-height moves between about 45 and 68 with fit size, and letter width scales between 0.78× (A = 1) and about 1.28× (A = 20). Sidebearing stays 9, word space 28.
  • Curves: each arc's radius is multiplied by A + cos 3(θ − rotation), normalised so the arc still starts and ends exactly where it joins a stem.
  • Bowls: closed bowls trace the curve itself, rotated by the rotation slider and stretched to the bowl's box. They're shared by every pass, so counters stay open.
  • Bent strokes: every straight stroke bows sideways by up to a fifth of its length, strongest at low A, with the direction set by the stroke's angle and the rotation slider. Ends stay put, so letters still meet the baseline.
  • Looped joins: corners sharper than 50° get a small loop of the curve tucked inside the angle.
  • Pen nib: copy i of the stack is the pen for pass i, so copies, rotation and fit size set stroke contrast and the inline effect. Off, every pass uses a round pen 10 units wide.
  • Stroke endings apply to free stroke ends only; ends that meet another stroke stay joined. Ball puts balls on curved ends and cuts stems flat, as most ball-terminal fonts do. Serifs on vertical strokes lie flat along the baseline, x-height or cap line; on horizontal strokes (T, E, Z arms) they stand upright. With Shape endings on: round ends, balls (a lobe pointing outward, like a teardrop), slabs, hairlines and wedges are copies of the shape; angled cuts turn with each pass's rotation; flares grow and tapers lengthen as A/B drops. Off, they are plain circles, rectangles, triangles, a fixed 30° cut, a fixed flare and a fixed taper.
  • In "Plain pen + ornaments" the reshaped skeleton is drawn with a round 10-unit stroke, bowls are filled with the stack, and stroke ends get small stacks as terminals. Joins and nib don't apply there.
  • Shape before the text: by default the mark is as tall as the text block — cap-height to baseline for one line, first cap-height to second baseline for two lines or more — enlarged 6% because a lobed shape looks smaller than a letter of the same height. It is centred on that block and set 0.6 of its radius away from the first letter. It is placed by its real outline rather than the curve's centre, so lopsided shapes still sit centred. The three sliders shift, raise or lower, and scale it from there. When the screen is too narrow for that without breaking words, the mark sits above the text instead, cap-height tall and left-aligned; then Distance moves it along the line and Height changes the space below it.
  • Copies are clamped at 1.6× the first one when fit size pushes the scale above 1.
